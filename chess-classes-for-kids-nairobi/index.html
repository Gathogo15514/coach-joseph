Chess teaches a child to slow down, look for the opponent's idea and take responsibility for every decision. Those habits carry into the classroom long before the first trophy.

I coach children every week at German School Nairobi, Strathmore School, Light International School and PMadol Chess Club, and take a small number of private students across Nairobi and the surrounding counties. Some are complete beginners. Others compete at Chess Kenya events, including the Kenya U17 National Chess Championship.

What a lesson looks like

  • Beginners learn how the pieces move, check and checkmate, and the simple tactics that win most children's games: forks, pins and undefended pieces.
  • Improving players work on opening principles, calculation and basic endgames, using positions from their own games.
  • Competitive juniors prepare for rated tournaments with an opening repertoire, timed games and a full review after every event.

Every child gets homework between sessions, and parents receive a short note on what was covered and what to practise.

Common questions

What age can my child start chess?

Most children are ready from about five years old, once they can sit and focus for 30 to 45 minutes. Younger children start with short, game-based lessons.

Does my child need to know how to play already?

No. Many students start as complete beginners and learn the moves in the first two or three sessions.

Where do lessons take place?

At your home or an agreed venue in Nairobi, Kiambu, Kajiado or Machakos, or online if that suits your family better.
